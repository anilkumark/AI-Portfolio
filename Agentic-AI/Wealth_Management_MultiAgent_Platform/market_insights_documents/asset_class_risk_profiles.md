# Asset Class Risk Profiles

**Document ID:** RISK-PROF-002
**Version:** 1.0
**Effective Date:** July 2026
**Review Date:** October 2026

## 1. Purpose

This document summarizes the relative volatility and risk characteristics of
each asset class used in portfolio risk scoring, for client-facing
explanation purposes.

## 2. Equities (Stocks)

Historically the most volatile of the core asset classes, equities offer the
highest long-term growth potential but the widest range of short-term price
swings. Sector and single-issuer concentration meaningfully amplifies
equity-specific risk.

## 3. Fixed Income (Bonds)

Bonds generally exhibit lower volatility than equities, with returns driven
primarily by interest-rate movements and issuer credit quality. Government
and high-grade corporate bonds are considered lower risk; high-yield
("junk") bonds carry meaningfully more volatility and credit risk.

## 4. Real Estate

Real estate offers moderate volatility with return characteristics distinct
from public equities, but carries elevated liquidity risk — positions cannot
always be exited quickly without a price concession.

## 5. Cash and Cash Equivalents

Cash and money-market instruments carry minimal volatility and are the
primary tool for reducing overall portfolio risk score, at the cost of
lower expected long-term returns.

## 6. Using This Profile in Client Conversations

When explaining a portfolio's risk score to a client, reference the asset
classes actually held and their relative volatility from this document,
rather than generic statements about "risk." This keeps advice grounded in
the specific composition of the client's holdings.
